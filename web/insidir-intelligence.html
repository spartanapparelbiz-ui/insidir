The instrument

One mark. Three ways in.

The dotted i is Insidir's mark and the way into Intelligence. Its three parts are its three experiences, in the order research usually goes: discover what changed, investigate it, understand it. What you do with it is yours to decide.

  1. Discover · the dot

    Briefing

    What changed for the companies and people you follow, and why each item is there.

  2. Investigate · the stem

    Investigate

    A research question in plain English, answered from SEC records with numbered sources.

  3. Understand · the base

    Mirror

    An institution's disclosed 13F holdings, and how a hypothetical budget would split by their weights.

01 · Briefing

What matters to you, and why it is there.

Your briefing is built from the companies on your watchlist and the people you follow. New Form 4 insider filings and company current reports (8-K) are grouped into stories: every Form 4 at one company disclosed on the same day is one story.

  1. 01

    Matched to you

    Only records about what you follow: your watchlist tickers, the people you follow and joint filings that list them.

  2. 02

    Ordered by published rules

    A person you follow, a company you watch, how recent, an open-market trade or an 8-K: each rule and its weight is listed beside the briefing. No investment score and no rating.

  3. 03

    Delivered your way

    Read it in the app, or turn on a browser notification for each new item, a daily digest or a weekly one.

Each story shows when the event happened, when it was disclosed and when Insidir retrieved it, with links to the SEC documents. Public disclosures can arrive days after the event.

02 · Investigate

Ask a question. See the sources.

Ask in plain English. Insidir works out which companies, people, record types and dates you mean, retrieves the matching SEC records and assembles an answer in which every sentence carries the numbers of the records it rests on. When the records cannot answer, it says so and lists what is missing.

Questions members ask

  • What did Nvidia insiders disclose this month?
  • What changed in Tesla's recent SEC filings?
  • Show me recent insider sales at Microsoft.
  • What companies have the executives I follow disclosed transactions in?
  • Compare Apple's recent disclosures with Microsoft's.
  • What changed since my last visit?

Questions that ask what to buy, sell or how much to invest are declined: Insidir explains what public filings say and leaves the decision to you.

03 · Mirror

Historical holdings, hypothetically.

Large investment managers disclose their U.S. stock holdings each quarter on Form 13F. Mirror shows a manager's latest disclosed holdings and how a budget you type would split in proportion to the reported values.

  1. 01

    A past filing

    13F holdings are reported up to 45 days after each quarter ends; the period and filing date are always shown.

  2. 02

    Plain arithmetic

    Long share positions only; options and notes are left out and listed. Every dollar is computed in code, never estimated by a model.

  3. 03

    Never a trade

    There is no brokerage connection, no order and no copy button. The result is labelled hypothetical.

04 · How to read it

Five labels. Always shown.

Everything in Intelligence says what kind of information it is, so a new record is never confused with an old one, or a fact with a reading of it.

  • New
    Newly received

    Retrieved since your last visit. The time Insidir retrieved it is shown beside the time it was disclosed.

  • Filed
    Historical disclosure

    A record with its event date and its filing date, which can be days apart.

  • Source
    Verified fact

    A figure taken from an SEC document, with a link to that document.

  • AI
    AI-written explanation

    Plain English written by a model from cited records only, checked for figures and citations before it is shown.

  • Hypothetical
    Simulation

    A calculation on a past filing, such as a Mirror allocation. Not a holding and not a trade.

05 · Where AI is used

Code for the facts. AI for the words.

Selecting records, totals, dates, comparisons and allocations are done in code. A model is used only to put cited records into plain English, and only when you ask.

Done in code

  • Which filings match what you follow
  • Transaction totals, counts and dates
  • Form 4 code classification
  • Comparisons between companies
  • Mirror's allocations

Written by a model, on request

  • A plain-English summary of one filing
  • A synthesis of an Investigate answer, citing its numbered sources

Checked before it is shown

  • Every figure must appear in the cited records
  • Every citation must be real
  • No advice, predictions or directives
  • A failed check shows the factual answer alone
