1. Who is responsible

Insidir is operated by Aethric LLC. This policy applies to insidir.com and the Insidir research services we provide.

We collect and use information to operate accounts and subscriptions, deliver source-linked research, maintain security and provide optional user-selected features.

2. What information we collect

  • Account information: your email address, authentication credentials in hashed form, and information you choose to provide when creating or using an account. Passwords are not stored in plain text.
  • Subscription information: your Stripe customer and subscription identifiers, the status of your subscription and the end of its current billing period. We do not receive or store your full card number.
  • Subscription agreement records: when you agree to the automatic-renewal terms before checkout, we record the terms shown, the offer, the date and time, and the network address and browser that sent the agreement. We also record billing events such as cancellations, refunds, disputes and failed payments.
  • Research data: stock tickers you add to a watchlist, people you follow, your private notes, saved review statuses, optional manually entered holdings and their update times.
  • Technical and security data: essential session cookies, request information and rate-limit counters needed to prevent abuse (stored as one-way digests, not as your email or network address). A security log records sign-ins, failed sign-ins, password changes and resets, email confirmations and account deletion, with the time, network address and browser; email addresses in it are kept only as one-way digests. You can see your own recent entries on the Account page.
  • Email and alerts: whether your address is confirmed; the alert settings you choose (in the app, by email immediately or as a daily digest, which kinds of filings); each time you turn alert emails on or off, with the consent text shown, the time, network address and browser; the alerts created for you; and a delivery log of emails we sent you (kind, time, delivery status). The text of security emails (which carry one-time links) and alert emails is deleted once they are delivered; subscription notices are kept as the record of what you were told.
  • Briefing and device notifications: your Briefing frequency (as it happens, daily or weekly) and the times of your last visits, so that New marks and digests are correct. If you allow device notifications, we store your browser's push subscription (the address of your browser's push service and the keys needed to encrypt messages to it) and a delivery log for each message (status, attempts and times). Messages are delivered through the push service your browser maker runs, such as Google, Mozilla, Apple or Microsoft, which receives each encrypted message and that address. Devices with notifications on may also receive occasional Insidir product announcements. You can turn notifications off in Alerts or in your browser's settings; deleting your account deletes its push subscriptions.
  • Product measurement: to learn whether visitors understand the product and can sign up without trouble, our own pages record a few named events on insidir.com itself: which page was shown, which trial button was pressed, whether a product example was opened, and, from our own records and Stripe's signed notifications, whether an account was created, a checkout started or completed, a subscription renewed or was cancelled, and whether a member used a feature for the first time. Each browser keeps a random visitor number in its local storage. When you create an account from that browser, the number is linked to your account so that we can count how many visits lead to a sign-up; it is never linked to your network address or device. No search text, research question, ticker, name or payment detail is recorded in these records, and the visitor number is never shared. If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, our pages record no visits or clicks and keep no visitor number; account events (an account created, a checkout started or completed, a renewal or cancellation) are still recorded from our own records, because they are part of running your subscription. These records are deleted after 13 months, and deleting your account removes your account from them.
  • Product analytics: to see how the site is used and where it gets in the way, most of our pages also send usage events to PostHog, an analytics provider (section 5): each page viewed and left and each link or button clicked, with the page's address cut to its path (no search terms, query string or fragment), the domain of the site that referred you, and your browser, operating system, device type and screen size. The text of what you click and everything you type are masked and never sent, and your screen and session are not recorded. A page address can name the public company, filing or reported insider the page is about. PostHog keeps a random identifier in your browser's local storage, not in a cookie; we do not link it to your account, your email address or the visitor number above. Sign-in, password, email-confirmation, account, billing, portfolio and administration pages send nothing to PostHog, and no page does if your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track.
  • Public-company information: SEC filings, reported insiders' names, transactions and other public records, which come from regulatory sources rather than from your personal account.

3. How information is used

We use account, subscription and research information to authenticate you, check that your subscription is active, display your saved work, show related public disclosures, secure the service, resolve errors and respond to support requests. We may review aggregated operational data to improve reliability.

Research notes and manual holdings are private to your signed-in account. We do not use them to place brokerage orders, and alerts are matched only against your watchlist and the people you follow, never against your holdings or notes.

We email you subscription notices (acknowledgment of your subscription terms, receipts, trial reminders, failed payments, cancellation confirmations), security notices (email confirmation, password resets and changes, account deletion) and, only if you turn them on, alert emails. Every alert email has a one-click unsubscribe link. Device notifications are sent only to browsers where you have allowed them.

4. Cookies and authentication

Insidir uses a first-party session cookie named insidir_sid. The cookie is marked HttpOnly and SameSite=Lax; it uses the Secure attribute on HTTPS production deployments. Session records normally expire after 14 days unless ended earlier.

Insidir does not use advertising or analytics cookies. The random visitor number used for product measurement and the identifier PostHog uses for product analytics (section 2) are kept in your browser's local storage, not in cookies.

5. Hosting, official sources, analytics and payments

We use infrastructure and database services to host the site and save information, including our cloud deployment provider. When you open a linked original SEC filing, that external site receives your request under its own privacy practices.

Email is delivered by our email provider (Postmark, when enabled), which receives your address and the message to deliver it and tells us whether it was delivered, bounced or marked as spam. A hard bounce or spam complaint stops further email to that address.

AI-assisted research. Insidir writes its research text by rules unless the operator switches AI-assisted wording on. When it is on, the text to be explained is sent to an AI provider: OpenRouter, which passes the request to the model's maker (currently OpenAI models), for filing summaries and Investigate answers, and Anthropic for explanations of individual records. What is sent is the public-record evidence being explained and, if you asked a question, the words of your question. An Investigate answer can include facts about the companies on your watchlist or the people you follow, so a request can show what you follow; it never includes your email address, password, payment details, notes or holdings. The providers process requests under their commercial terms.

Accepted AI text is stored by Insidir and reused for the same request, with its sources, and a usage record (feature, model, token counts, cost and outcome, linked to your account) is kept to enforce spending limits. Investigate questions are not saved to your account: an answer is kept in server memory for about two minutes so that a page refresh can show it.

Payments are processed by Stripe. When you subscribe, we send Stripe your email address and an internal account identifier to create your customer record, and you enter your payment details on Stripe's checkout page, under Stripe's own privacy terms. Stripe tells us the status of your subscription so that we can give or remove access. Updating your card and viewing invoices happen in Stripe's customer portal; you can cancel from Billing in Insidir or in that portal.

Product analytics are processed by PostHog (PostHog, Inc., on its United States cloud), which receives the usage events described in section 2 and, like any website you connect to, your network address with each request. PostHog estimates an approximate location (country and city) from that address; we have it set not to store the address itself. PostHog processes these events on our behalf under its terms and privacy policy. Because they are not linked to your account, deleting your account does not remove them; PostHog keeps them for the retention period of our plan with it.

6. Public logos and portraits

Company research screens display each company's published logo and published photographs of its officers and directors. Logos come from Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons, public stock-logo libraries and the company's own website. Photos come from Wikimedia Commons, the company's own leadership or board pages, and headshots printed in SEC proxy statements (the company's own, or another company's where the same person also serves as a director, identified by their SEC filer number). Insidir fetches these images on its own servers, prepares them for display (for example removing a background tile or cropping a portrait) and serves them from insidir, so viewing a company report does not send your browser's request to those providers. Some other screens still load a few images directly from Wikimedia Commons, which may receive basic browser request information, including your IP address.

These are authentic published images, not generated or inferred ones. A photo is shown only when the person's full name matches an SEC reporting owner and the source ties that person to the company; a surname alone is never enough, and otherwise the person's initials are shown. Each photo names its source when you hover over it. Trademarks and photographs belong to their owners and are used only to identify the company or person. To report a wrong or outdated image, or to ask us to remove one, contact hello@aethric.llc.

7. Optional brokerage connections

Brokerage linking is optional and disabled unless explicitly enabled with an approved provider integration. When available, read-only access requires your consent through the provider. Insidir's server encrypts stored connection tokens and fetches holdings on demand. The integration cannot place trades.

You can use Insidir's manual holdings feature without linking a brokerage. Read-only access may still reveal sensitive financial information, so do not authorize a connection unless you understand what you are sharing.

8. Security, retention and deletion

We use access controls, protected session tokens, password hashing and encrypted brokerage credentials where those features apply. No internet service can promise absolute security.

We keep account and research information while it is needed to provide the service or comply with applicable requirements. When you delete your account, we first cancel any subscription that could still be charged, then remove account-linked saved data from our main database, subject to necessary backups, technical retention and legal obligations. Stripe keeps its customer record and invoices under its own terms and for accounting.

Kept after deletion: records of your agreement to the subscription terms and of billing events are kept for at least three years, or one year after your subscription ends if that is longer, with your email address but without a link to a deleted account, because the law requires proof of that agreement and they may be needed to resolve a dispute. Records of your agreement to receive alert emails, and changes to it, are kept the same way. Administrative actions on the service are logged for security without your personal data. AI usage records stay, without a link to the deleted account, for cost accounting. Security log entries are deleted after 180 days; one entry recording the deletion of your account is kept for that period with your email address only as a one-way digest.

9. Your choices and rights

You can read Insidir's public pages without creating an account. Signed-in users can update or remove saved holdings, watchlists and followed people with the product controls, cancel their subscription from Billing, change alert emails from Alerts, download a copy of their data and delete their account from Account. Anyone can stop product measurement and product analytics by turning on Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track in their browser.

Depending on where you live, you may have additional rights to access, correct, delete or limit processing of personal information. Contact us for requests the product does not handle directly.

10. Young users and policy changes

Insidir is not directed to children under 13. Users who are not legally able to agree to these terms on their own must obtain any required parent or guardian consent.

We may change this policy as features, integrations or legal requirements evolve. The effective date on this page will be updated when we do.
