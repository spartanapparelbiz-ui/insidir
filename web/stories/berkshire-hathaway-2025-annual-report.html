Berkshire's 2025 earnings fell by a quarter. Most of the change was not the businesses.

Berkshire Hathaway reported net earnings attributable to shareholders of $66,968 million for 2025, down from $88,995 million. Divide the share price by that number and you get one multiple. The 10-K itself breaks the total into insurance, a railroad, an energy company, manufacturing and retail, and investment gains, and says the gains are generally meaningless for judging a single year.

Where the quarter went

Net earnings fell −24.8%. Investment gains alone were $10,821 million lower than in 2024, and 2025 carried an $8,255 million impairment of the Kraft Heinz and Occidental investments that 2024 did not. Together that is $19,076 million of a $22,027 million decline.

The six other lines in the same table (underwriting, investment income, BNSF, Berkshire Hathaway Energy, manufacturing, service and retailing, and other) add up to $44,486 million against $47,437 million: −6.2%. That sum is Insidir's arithmetic on the filed table, not a figure the company reports.

What the company says about its own gains

The 10-K is direct about it:

“We believe that investment gains and losses, whether realized from dispositions or unrealized from changes in market prices and exchange rates, are generally meaningless in understanding our reported periodic results or evaluating our periodic economic performance.”

That is why a price-to-earnings ratio on Berkshire's reported net earnings moves with the stock market as much as with the businesses. It is one number standing in for very different ones.

Many businesses behind one ticker

  • Insurance: underwriting profit and investment income are reported separately, and the float (about $176 billion) is money held for policyholders that Berkshire invests.
  • BNSF: a freight railroad with about 35,000 employees at the end of 2025.
  • Berkshire Hathaway Energy: a holding company whose domestic regulated energy interests include four regulated U.S. utility companies.
  • Manufacturing, service and retailing: the largest operating line in 2025, at $13,647 million after tax.

Questions to ask of any filing like this

  1. Which part of reported earnings comes from operations, and which from market prices?
  2. Which businesses are regulated, and how does that change what their earnings mean?
  3. What does the segment note (Note 26) show that a single multiple hides?
  4. How did each line move, not just the total?
