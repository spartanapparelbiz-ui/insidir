1. What Insidir is

Insidir is financial research software operated by Aethric LLC. It organizes public records, mainly SEC filings: it checks supported filing sources on a schedule, matches new records to the companies and people you follow, prepares briefings and alerts, and lets you investigate the evidence and keep private notes.

Aethric LLC is not registered as an investment adviser with the SEC or any state, is not a broker-dealer or a member of FINRA or SIPC, and does not hold customer money or securities. Insidir does not provide personalized investment advice, does not recommend that any person buy, sell or hold any security, and does not place, route or execute orders. These limits are enforced in the software, not only stated here: requests for personalized recommendations or order placement are refused by the server.

Insidir is not affiliated with, approved by or endorsed by the SEC or any other government agency. Public SEC records are used as published.

2. Investment risk

Investing in securities involves risk, including the loss of the money you invest. Past transactions, including purchases or sales reported by company insiders, do not predict future prices or results. Nothing on Insidir is a guarantee of any outcome.

An insider purchase or sale can happen for many reasons unrelated to a company’s prospects, such as taxes, diversification, pre-arranged trading plans (Rule 10b5-1) or compensation. Make your own decisions, and consider speaking with a licensed professional who knows your circumstances.

Insidir is not insured against investment losses, and no protection of any kind applies to decisions you make using it.

3. Public records, not nonpublic information

“Insider trading” on Insidir means transactions that company officers, directors and 10% owners are legally required to report to the SEC on Form 4, which the SEC publishes on EDGAR. These are public reports, available to everyone. Insidir does not have, seek or provide material nonpublic information, and trading on such information can be illegal.

4. Data accuracy and reporting delays

  • Delays. Insiders generally file Form 4 within two business days of a trade, and Insidir checks the SEC's feed of new Form 4 filings about every 2 minutes when its import is running; checks can be delayed or fail, so what you see can lag the actual transaction by days. Each line shows its trade date and the date it was filed.
  • Prices. Market prices come from Yahoo Finance and are delayed; they are not suitable for trading.
  • Coverage. The import covers recent filings and can miss filings that fail to parse or that EDGAR publishes late. A missing record does not mean nothing was filed.
  • Errors and amendments. Filers make mistakes and amend filings (Form 4/A). Insidir shows amendments and counts a restated line once, but cannot correct a filer’s own errors. Confirm anything important against the linked original filing.
  • Calculated figures. Values are shares multiplied by the price the filer reported. Market capitalization, enterprise value and similar figures are calculated from the cited sources and show their inputs.

To report an error, email hello@aethric.llc.

5. Automated and AI-generated content

Insidir automatically checks supported public filing sources, indexes records, groups related events, and can generate watchlist briefings and notification digests according to member preferences. Coverage, update frequency and delivery are not guaranteed: public filings may arrive late, processing may fail, and push delivery depends on device permissions and third-party services. An alert is not a trading signal or a guarantee that every event will be detected.

Research text may be assembled deterministically from records or, where an AI provider is configured and the feature is available, supplemented by AI-assisted explanations of cited evidence. AI output may be incomplete, inaccurate or misleading even after automated checks. Always verify original records, dates, figures and reporting periods. Insidir does not provide personalized buy, sell or hold recommendations or execute trades. Hypothetical portfolio allocations are illustrations, not actual holdings or performance.

How AI-assisted text is checked. When the operator switches it on, Insidir can ask a third-party AI model, through OpenRouter (currently OpenAI models) or Anthropic, to put cited records into plain English: a summary of one filing, the wording of an Investigate answer, or an explanation of one record, answering your question if you asked one. The model receives that evidence and your question, never your account details (see the Privacy policy). Every statement must cite the numbered source it relies on, and the server checks the answer before it is shown: invented citations, figures that do not match the cited source, buy/sell/hold language, promises and statements about why someone traded or where a price will go are rejected, and the rules-written answer is shown instead. AI-written text is labelled as such, with its sources and the time they were retrieved. Questions asking what you personally should buy, sell, hold or invest are refused without being sent to a model. AI output can still be wrong; check it against the linked filing.

Historical holdings (Mirror). Institutional investment managers file Form 13F up to 45 days after the end of each calendar quarter, and it reports their holdings on the last day of that quarter, so it is weeks old when it appears. A 13F covers only certain U.S.-listed securities and long positions; it leaves out short positions, cash and most other assets, may omit holdings the SEC has allowed to be kept confidential, and can be amended. Insidir covers a selected set of managers, not every filer. A Mirror allocation applies a budget you type to those past reported weights, using long share positions only: it is a hypothetical calculation, not an actual portfolio, a record of performance, investment advice, or a way to copy any manager's trades. Insidir places no orders.

6. Conflicts of interest and affiliate relationships

  • How Insidir is paid. Insidir earns money only from subscriptions. It does not receive payment from the companies, insiders or securities it covers, does not sell order flow, and is not paid to feature any security.
  • Creator partners. Insidir pays some content creators a one-time commission of $10 when someone they refer becomes a paying subscriber. Partners are required to disclose that relationship clearly in their content, as the FTC’s Endorsement Guides require. A creator’s opinions about securities are their own, not Insidir’s.
  • Personal holdings. People who work on Insidir may own securities that appear in it. Insidir’s pages are generated from public records the same way for every company and do not reflect anyone’s holdings.
