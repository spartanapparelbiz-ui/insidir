How it works

Follow once. Insidir keeps working.

Seven steps, shown on a real filing: PepsiCo director Robert C. Pohlad's Form 4, filed with the SEC on October 16, 2026. Filings reach the SEC after the trade, and checks and notifications can be delayed, so nothing here is instant or exhaustive.

Step 01 · Follow

Choose what to watch, once.

Save companies to your watchlist and follow the officers, directors and major owners who report to the SEC. That is the only setup.

  • Companies: any company in the SEC's ticker list.
  • People: anyone who files a Form 4, followed across every company they report for.
  • Changes later: add or remove either at any time.

Step 02 · Monitor

Supported sources, checked on a schedule.

Insidir reads the SEC's own feeds at set intervals. It does not watch every market or every source, and a check can be late or fail; when that happens the product shows the last successful import.

  • Form 4 insider filings: the SEC's current-filings feed, about every 15 minutes.
  • 8-K and 6-K current reports: the SEC's current-reports feed about every 15 minutes, plus a direct check of each watched company every few hours.
  • 13F holdings: a selected set of institutions, about twice a day.

Step 04 · Organize

Four lines, one finding.

This filing reports four separate lines. Insidir reads each one from the filed XML, keeps the SEC's transaction code, and groups them into a single finding with the totals.

  • Code P is the SEC's code for a purchase. Awards, exercises, gifts and tax withholdings are kept apart from purchases and sales.
  • Value is shares multiplied by the price the filer reported.

Your Briefing, in a published order.

Findings for what you follow are assembled into your Briefing, as they arrive, daily or weekly. The order comes from published rules, shown with every item. There is no rating and no score for any security.

  • Every source attached: the filing, its accession number and when it was retrieved.

Step 07 · Investigate

The code is a start. The filing is the evidence.

Open the finding and every line leads to the original document on EDGAR. Here, the filing's own footnote changes the reading: these "purchases" moved shares from the director's irrevocable trusts in exchange for cash, rather than buying them on the market.

  • Insidir shows the reported code, dates, shares and prices, and links the filing.
  • You read the footnotes and decide what it means. Insidir does not interpret them for you.

Questions

Good to know.

Anything else? Write to hello@aethric.llc.

What does Insidir do automatically?

It checks supported SEC sources on a schedule (Form 4 insider filings, 8-K and 6-K company reports, and selected institutions' 13F holdings), matches new records to the companies and people you follow, groups them into findings, builds your Briefing and sends alerts the way you choose. You read the evidence and decide.

How quickly do new filings appear?

Form 4 filings are checked about every 15 minutes, so most appear soon after the SEC publishes them, but not instantly and not guaranteed. Insiders have 2 business days to file a Form 4 after a trade, and an institution can file a 13F up to 45 days after the quarter ends. Checks can be delayed or fail; the product shows when it last imported.

Will I be notified about everything?

No system can promise that. Alerts cover records Insidir supports and has imported. In-app alerts are on by default; device push and email are opt-in and depend on your settings, your device and outside services, so a notification can be late or missing. Always check the app.

Is this insider information?

No. Everything on Insidir is public: a Form 4 is a report an insider files with the SEC after a trade, and anyone can read it on EDGAR. Insidir organises those public records; it has no nonpublic information.

Does Insidir use AI?

Briefings and investigation answers are assembled from records by rules. Insidir can also add AI-assisted explanations of cited records where that feature is switched on; that text is labelled AI, must cite its sources and is checked before it is shown, and it can still be wrong. See the Disclosures and Privacy policy.

What is Mirror?

A way to explore selected institutions' disclosed 13F holdings, compare quarters and see how a hypothetical budget would split by their reported weights. A 13F describes holdings at the end of a past quarter, so it is weeks old when filed. Mirror never copies trades, places orders or shows real performance.

Does Insidir execute trades or give advice?

No. Insidir is a financial information and research service, not a broker-dealer or investment adviser. It does not place trades, hold money or make personalized investment recommendations.

How much does it cost, and how do I cancel?

$1 at checkout for a 3-day trial, then $39 a month in US dollars until you cancel. One plan, every tool; one trial per account. Cancel from Billing in one step; access continues to the end of the period you paid for, and cancelling during the trial means the $39 is never charged.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. Every page works on a phone, and you can add Insidir to your Home Screen from your browser's share or menu button to open it like an app and receive device notifications where your phone supports them.
